The maps incorporate NASA imagery and elevation data. Solar System Scope
notes that unmapped gaps may be reconstructed and that its source colors
are enhanced. These are reference composites, not live spacecraft images.

Adaptations for this portfolio: resized and compressed to WebP, mapped to
rotating spheres, shaded from an unrendered solar light source, and exposed
for a dim space background. Earth uses separate day, city-light, and cloud
maps with a thin atmosphere. No endorsement by the source creators or NASA
is implied.

Earth and the Moon are the only rendered celestial bodies. The Moon's
radius is 1737.4/6371 of Earth's. Its eccentricity is 0.0549, with an
Earth-facing surface and prograde orbit. The orbit is projected at
68 degrees for this camera view;
this is not a claim that the Moon's ecliptic inclination is 68 degrees.

Orbital distance is compressed to 2.45 Earth radii (the real average is
about 60.3), and a lunar orbit takes 180 display seconds. Earth's rotation
is accelerated independently. Apparent sizes, distances, exposure and
camera framing are composed for readability rather than being to scale.
Phases follow the solar-light/body vectors in this miniature
scene; this external viewpoint is not the view of phases from Earth's
surface. This is an astronomical illustration, not an ephemeris or a
physical simulation of Solar System distances and orbital periods.
